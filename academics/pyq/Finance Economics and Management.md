#### Module III · Management (10 Marks)
**Case Study: Infosys and the Evolution of Management Style**  
In its early years, Infosys operated with a relatively autocratic management style. Decisions were centralized among the founders, which helped the company maintain strict control, speed in execution, and consistent quality during its growth phase. However, as the company expanded globally, this rigid style began to show limitations—employee morale and innovation suffered because decision-making was too top-heavy.  
In response, Infosys gradually adopted a democratic and participative style of management. Leaders began to involve employees more in project-level decisions, encouraged feedback sessions, and introduced open-door policies. This shift improved collaboration, built trust, and made Infosys a more attractive place to work.  
At the same time, leaders like Narayana Murthy displayed transformational leadership—emphasizing values, ethics, and a vision of creating a globally respected Indian IT company. By inspiring employees to look beyond short-term targets and align with the company’s mission, Infosys built a culture of ownership and excellence. This journey shows how an organization can adapt its management style over time to remain competitive, innovative, and people-focused.  
*Attempt any two:*
1. Why was an autocratic management style useful in the early stages of Infosys, and why did it become a limitation later?
2. As a manager, how would you balance democratic participation with the need for quick decision-making in a fast-paced IT environment?
3. Do you think transformational leadership is more sustainable than autocratic leadership in knowledge-based industries like IT? Why or why not?

#### 2nd Half · Module III: Management (16 Marks)
**Part A: Answer any one case study (10 Marks).**

**Case Study 1: The Strategic Reinvention of Nokia**  
Once the undisputed leader of the global mobile phone industry, Nokia lost its competitive edge during the smartphone revolution. Its failure to anticipate the dominance of iOS and Android, combined with organizational rigidity and late technological adaptation, resulted in a steep decline in market share. In 2013, Nokia sold its mobile handset business to Microsoft—a move initially seen as an exit from its core market. However, the company redefined its corporate identity by realigning toward network infrastructure, digital connectivity, and technology licensing. In following years, Nokia acquired Alcatel-Lucent to strengthen its position in network solutions, invested heavily in 5G research, and divested non-core operations to streamline its portfolio. Despite these shifts, it continues to face fierce competition from Huawei, Ericsson, and Samsung in the telecom equipment market. The strategic dilemma now lies in balancing technological innovation and financial sustainability while responding to global supply chain challenges and evolving market demands.  
*Questions:*  
(a) Evaluate Nokia’s transformation using retrenchment, turnaround, and renewal strategies. To what extent did these align with its redefined corporate mission?  
(b) Suggest a strategic combination approach for Nokia to sustain competitiveness in the 5G and AI-driven ecosystem. Should the company pursue vertical integration, strategic alliances, or diversification? Substantiate your recommendation with analytical reasoning.

*OR*

**Case Study 2: Reliance Industries: From Petrochemicals to Platform Power**  
Reliance Industries Ltd. (RIL) has undergone one of the most ambitious corporate transformations in India’s business history. Traditionally known for its dominance in petrochemicals and refining, RIL diversified into telecommunications (Jio) and retail in the early 2000s, building two of India’s largest consumer-facing businesses within a decade. By 2020, RIL’s digital ecosystem and retail network had become key drivers of growth, significantly contributing to profitability. However, the company’s strategic shift also involved calibrated retrenchment—divesting several non-core assets in oil and gas exploration and reducing capital exposure in slower-growth segments. Simultaneously, RIL made aggressive investments in renewable energy, including solar, hydrogen, and energy storage technologies, with the goal of becoming net carbon-zero by 2035. This simultaneous pursuit of growth, retrenchment, and green transformation reflects an intricate combination strategy, positioning RIL as both a traditional industrial leader and a digital-technology conglomerate.  
*Questions:*  
(a) Using Ansoff’s Product-Market Growth Matrix, analyze how Reliance’s diversification into telecom, retail, and renewable energy has expanded its strategic scope while leveraging existing competencies.  
(b) Critically assess whether selective divestment in fossil-based businesses represents strategic flexibility or risk mitigation in response to sustainability trends. What challenges might arise in managing such a diversified corporate portfolio?
